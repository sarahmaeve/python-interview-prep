# Analysis Training Rubric

Score the investigation process separately from whether the final tests pass.
Use the same rubric for self-review, peer review, and facilitated sessions.

## Scale

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | The behavior was absent or actively obstructed the investigation. |
| 1 | The behavior appeared with substantial prompting or inconsistency. |
| 2 | The behavior was competent and independently useful. |
| 3 | The behavior was strong, economical, and clearly explained. |

## Dimensions

| Dimension | Evidence of a strong performance |
|---|---|
| Report interpretation | Separates direct observations, reported claims, correlations, assumptions, and unknowns. |
| Clarification | Asks a few questions that materially narrow scope or distinguish likely causes. |
| Hypothesis management | Keeps plausible alternatives and revises them when evidence changes. |
| Evidence selection | Chooses focused, low-cost investigations and predicts what their outcomes would mean. |
| Localization | Finds the first contract violation instead of changing only where the symptom appears. |
| Repair scope | Makes a restrained, maintainable change justified by the evidence. |
| Verification | Reproduces the report, checks the repair directly, and runs appropriate regressions. |
| Communication | Gives a concise causal account, states certainty honestly, and respects component boundaries. |

The maximum score is 24. The number is useful for comparing a learner's own
attempts over time, not for asserting a universal interview cutoff.

Do not award points for asking the largest number of questions, running every
available command, guessing the intended patch, or matching the canonical
solution exactly.
